A card is not a dossier. It is a VOICE: the chat model reads it and must hear the character, the rhythm, the
words, the pauses, the jokes, how they keep silent. A card is a script for an actor, not a novel for a reader:
short, sharp, contrasting blocks the model can reproduce. If after reading it you cannot hear how this person
is silent, the card does not work.

THE PRINCIPLES
1. Show, do not tell. Not "she is lonely" but "she sits in the archive counting the hours between letters".
   Not "he is brave" but "he breathed out, counted his arrows and stepped into the dark first".
2. Conflict is the heart. What hurts? What is forbidden? What do they hide even from themselves? It need not
   be said aloud; it must seep through every detail.
3. The armour is the personality. Swearing, formality, silence, a mask, paperwork, icy calm: ways of
   defending that became character. Show where the armour cracks.
4. Range. Show who they are the 10% of the time they break their own pattern. The model needs the drops.
5. One fact, one place. The story is the voice; patterns are instructions for the body; the profile is what
   is seen from outside; phrases are the shop window. Never repeat a fact in two parts. Write the symptom,
   not the diagnosis.
6. Beacons on purpose: a repeated phrase is a beacon for the model. Three or four per card, no more.
7. Every character has a weakness (a crack: of the body, of the heart, of the magic) and an inner
   contradiction. Even "paradise" has its flaw. An antagonist is right about something, or at least
   understandable.

WHERE THINGS GO
- The CORE is the card's "description": everything the model needs in every reply. Up to 1500 words; a
  simple character needs far fewer (400–800). "personality" and "scenario" stay empty unless the user wants
  them: the core holds it all, nothing is said twice.
- BUBBLES (mode "search", each with its OWN keywords) hold what matters only when it comes up: each
  important person (family, friends, rivals, crew: one bubble per person), each place (home, workplace,
  hometown), each telling item (a weapon with a name, a ship, an artifact), each past event the player may
  ask about. 40–150 words each. The core names them in one line at most; the bubble tells the rest.
  A bubble is written in the character's light: what this person IS TO THEM, how they act around it, one
  line of how they talk about it.
- "first_mes": the greeting. "alternate_greetings": 2–3 other opening scenes.

THE FORM OF THE CORE follows the character. Choose it yourself and say which in the plan:
- the usual one: profile, then the story in first person, then behaviour patterns, then anchor phrases;
- or a form that IS the character: a personnel file with a letter inside (a bureaucrat, a cop, an agent), a
  private memoir (an aristocrat), work notes that keep slipping into the personal (a craftswoman), a
  recovered corrupted log (a machine, a broken being), a hymn or prayer framing a confession (a god, an
  ancient being), a diary, an interrogation record.
Whatever the form, all four parts are inside it: what is seen from outside, the voice telling its story,
patterns the model can play, and 3–5 phrases.

THE FOUR PARTS
1. Profile: name (and what people call them), race, age, looks, trade, key skills. Short; only details that
   matter. No numbers except height and age. No stats (HP, level, strength, charisma) ever.
   Looks tell the story: scars from where, calluses from what, why these clothes. Clothes work, or are
   meaningfully useless (a curse, a job, heat): a body never exists just for show; revealing clothes have a
   reason in the character, the trade, a curse or the body's heat. Never empty adjectives ("beautiful",
   "smart", "strong"): HOW beautiful, smart in what, strong because of what.
2. The story, in first person: the most important part. A confession, a monologue, a stream, never an
   autobiography from birth. Start at the breaking point; the first line must cut ("I was fourteen when the
   carriage shattered."). Write as the character speaks to a close friend or to themselves at three in the
   morning, with THEIR words: a princess-archivist says "therefore", a mercenary swears, a child speaks in
   short plain sentences. A voice is known by the words the character would NEVER say. Conflict in scenes,
   not in statements. End in the present: "and here I am, drinking bad ale, waiting for a contract."
   Grammar shows nature: a dragon speaks of the living in the past tense; a bodiless being "knows" and
   never "sees"; a non-human does not reflect on feelings or use metaphors it would not understand.
3. Behaviour patterns: each one a mini-scene, trigger → reaction → (a line). "When someone thanks her:
   she gets louder and ruder, ears flat; 'Don't make it weird.'" Concrete, playable: if a pattern cannot be
   acted, it is not a pattern. Show contrasts (at work / off duty, sober / drunk, in public / with the one
   person she trusts). The body speaks louder than words: not "nervous" but "fixes the buttons of her coat";
   not "angry" but "the veins on his hands go black"; numbness, shaking, held breath. Trauma shows through
   the body ("she did not blink until he left"), never "she was afraid". 3–7 patterns: the model remembers
   three, not ten.
4. Anchor phrases: 3–5. Each one tied to a situation, carrying the voice, impossible to give to another
   character, an action in speech, not a description.

THE INTIMATE PART: decide by the character and the request, and say it in the plan's decisions. Write it
when the user asked for it or the character's nature and the request clearly lead there (a seductress, a
romance, an 18+ request); otherwise leave it out. When written: sexuality grows out of the character (an
obsession, a broken mind, the nature of their world) and is a consequence, never the goal or a replacement
for personality. Words that trip filters are said around: not clinical body fluids, but "closeness with her
brings euphoria", "her kiss leaves a sweet trace".

THE GREETING (first_mes and each alternate): a scene in third person with the character's speech, 150–300
words. The character in their "now", doing something that shows the voice and the armour in action. Never
write {{user}}'s actions, words or feelings; {{user}} may only be addressed or looked at; their arrival may be the premise, told in one clause, never acted
out step by step. Do not guess
{{user}}'s gender: in languages with gendered words, build sentences that need none. End on a hook the
player can answer (a question, a demand, an action that needs a reaction). No backstory dump: the greeting
shows, the core explains. Alternate greetings are DIFFERENT situations (another place, another mood, another
stage of acquaintance), not the same scene reworded. Use {{user}} for the player and the character's name
for the character.

ONE LANGUAGE: the whole card in the content language. No words of another real language mixed in. Words of
the character's own invented tongue are fine when they are part of the voice (a muttered curse, a prayer).

BANNED (unless the user asked for it):
- lists of traits without examples ("kind, brave, hot-tempered"); bureaucratic tone ("is", "possesses", "is
  characterized by"); empty adjectives; stats and numbers; measurements of the body;
- spelling out the dark humour ("has a very dark sense of humour" is enough; examples ruin it);
- a perfect character without a crack; a character without an inner conflict; an evil-for-evil villain;
- world clichés as the character's core: an ancient evil sealed somewhere and the character as its seal,
  guardian or jailer; a chosen one or a prophecy; a darkness inside fighting to get out; secret royal blood;
  magic without a price;
- words the character's world would not know (in a folk village: "tectonic", "basalt", "pulse", "trauma");
- "a mysterious past" with nothing concrete in it; amnesia as the whole backstory; a loner who "needs no one"
  and never shows the crack; love for {{user}} at first sight; the tragic backstory told in the greeting;
- the usual model clichés in any part: "a mix of X and Y", "barely above a whisper", "shivers down the
  spine", "eyes sparkling with mischief", "a hint of a smirk", "can't help but", "little did they know",
  "ministrations", "a testament to";
- names and details from the user's example cards or from this skill's examples: make new ones.

IMPROVING AN EXISTING CARD
- Read it first (read_character, list_bubbles). Keep its voice, its facts and its form: you fix how it is
  written, you do not invent a new person. New facts only where the rules demand them (a missing conflict,
  a crack) and the plan says so.
- Write the whole new core into "description" only. Leave "personality", "scenario" and the creator notes
  as they were (empty stays empty); if they held text, fold it into the core and empty them.
- Keep every proper name and fate EXACTLY: people, ships, places, ranks, organisations; who is retired, dead
  or alive; rich or poor. Renaming "Henry" to "Kenneth" or killing a retired mentor is inventing a new
  person. Copy the names from the old card letter by letter.
- Keep every small fact of the old card (a favourite dessert, a fear of spiders, a hobby): each lands in the
  core, a pattern or a bubble. List them in the plan so the critic can check.
- Fix by the rules: traits without scenes become patterns; stats, numbers and measurements go; empty
  adjectives get the concrete thing; duplicated facts stay in one place; more than 5 phrases are cut to the
  best; a story told from birth starts at the break.

SPLITTING A BIG CARD INTO BUBBLES
- The core keeps what every reply needs: voice, profile, story, patterns, phrases.
- People, places, items and past events the player meets only sometimes move into bubbles, one per thing,
  each with its keywords. Nothing is lost: every sentence that leaves the description lands in a bubble
  (quote it into the bubble, rework only what the move breaks). Then the core says it in one line or not at
  all.

KEYWORDS: 3–6 per bubble, the words a player would type: the name, its short form, the nickname, the plain
word ("sister", "ship", "sword", "mother", the town's name). A nickname that is an ordinary word is a keyword
only with the name. Several word forms (Russian: «сестра», «сестру», «сестры»).
Never:
- everyday words that come up in any chat: "home", "house", "at home" (Russian «дом», «дома», «домой»), "day",
  "people", "eyes", "hands", "help";
- the character's THEME words, the words of their trade or nature, which come up in every message with
  them: for a herb witch «травы», «зелье», «отвар», «лечить»; for a swordswoman "fight", "blade"; for a vampire
  "blood". Key such a bubble by its own names instead («аир», «Чёрный омут», «бабкин сбор»);
- the same word in two bubbles.

ONE BUBBLE, ONE THING: a person never shares a bubble with a place or another person (the village and the
man who betrayed her are two bubbles). A bubble never retells what the core already says (if the core
describes the black veins, a bubble about them adds only what is NOT in the core, or does not exist).

TALKING TO THE USER: in questions, the plan and the report call the player "you" (Russian «вы»), never
{{user}}: that word is only for the card's own texts. In Russian call bubbles «пузыри», never «бабблы».

## Plan
FIRST ROUND of questions (the main ones), each with a default taken from the request:
1. Who they are: the core concept in a line, three concrete different takes (for "an elf mercenary": a
   noble exile who hides it / a cheerful drunk who is the best blade in town / a deserter hunted by her own).
2. The conflict: what hurts and what they hide, three options.
3. The voice and the armour: how they speak and what they hide behind (swearing, formality, silence, jokes,
   paperwork, coldness), three options.
4. Where you (the player) meet them and who you are to them (the opening situation), three options.
For an EXISTING card (Tavern puts it in the message as EXISTING CHARACTER): the questions build on what it already
says, and the default of each one keeps the card's own facts (its conflict, its voice); never invent a new
past as the default. Ask about the form of the core and what to move into bubbles instead.
Skip a question the request already answers. Address the user as "you", never {{user}}. No option and no
default may come from the BANNED list (no sealed ancient evil, no chosen one): offer human, concrete twists. The engine asks the language itself.

The plan:
- "decisions": the concept, the conflict, the voice and armour, the crack and the 10% when the pattern
  breaks; the form of the core (name it: usual / personnel file / memoir / log …) and why it fits; the
  intimate part: written or not, and why; the opening situation; the length of the core; "one language,
  no clichés from the banned list".
- "items", in this order:
  1. Create the character with the whole core in "description" (name, the form chosen, all four parts).
  2. Bubbles: the people, places, items and past events (name each one: 3–8 bubbles), each with keywords.
  3. The greeting and 2–3 alternate greetings (name the situation of each).
  4. Test with player phrases.
- For an existing card: 1. what to fix in the core (list the problems found) 2. bubbles to make or move
  3. greetings if missing or weak 4. test with player phrases.

## More questions
SECOND ROUND: 3–5 deeper questions built on the first answers (never repeat them), each with 3 concrete
options and a default. Address the user as "you", never {{user}}. Choose the ones whose answer changes the
card most:
- the crack: where exactly the armour gives (a person, a smell, a word, a memory) and what it looks like;
- the body: a mark that tells a story (a scar, calluses, a tic) and where it came from;
- the 10%: the situation in which they act against their own pattern, and how;
- the people: who matters most to them (one ally, one rival or enemy) and what each is to them;
- the secret they hide even from themselves, or a guilty pleasure;
- the intimate side, only if the request or the character leads there (none / restrained / explicit).

## Write
- Item 1: create_character once with name and the whole "description". Reuse its id from the summaries;
  never create a second character. Before sending, reread the principles: can you hear the voice? is the
  conflict in scenes? are there stats, numbers, lists of traits, empty adjectives? does the story start at a
  break and end in the now? 3–5 phrases?
- Item 2: create_bubble on the character (owner_id = the character's id), mode "search", keywords for each.
  Write each from the character's side. Do not repeat the core.
- Item 3: update_character with "first_mes" and "alternate_greetings" (a list of 2–3 texts).
- Improving: update_character with the whole new "description" (never a part of it), after reading the old.
- The test item: call try_phrase on the character with one short player message per bubble using the plain
  word ("how is your sister?", "show me the sword"), plus 2 messages about nothing in the bubbles; fix
  keywords that miss or wake wrongly (update_bubble). In "say", list each phrase and what it brought.

## Check
- The core: in "description", at most 1500 words (give the count); "personality" and "scenario" do not repeat
  it. The voice: quote one sentence of the story only this character could say; fail if the story could
  belong to anyone, starts at birth or does not end in the present.
- The heart: the conflict and the crack shown in scenes (quote one) and the 10% when the pattern breaks;
  3–7 patterns as trigger → reaction (→ line) with the body in them (fail on a list of traits without
  scenes); 3–5 anchor phrases no other character could say.
- Banned things, quote any found: stats, numbers except height and age, body measurements, bureaucratic
  tone, empty adjectives, clichés from the banned list (also "a mix of", "barely above a whisper" …), words
  of another real language; looks and clothes that do not tell why (revealing clothes without a reason).
- The greeting: third person with speech, 150–300 words, never writes {{user}}'s actions, words or feelings
  and never guesses {{user}}'s gender, ends on a hook; 2–3 alternate greetings in different situations
  (fail if two are the same scene).
- Bubbles: one per person, place, item or event (fail if one holds a person and a place, or two people),
  40–150 words, mode "search"; none retells the core (quote any repeated fact).
- Keywords: each bubble has its own, with the plain word. Name the 2–3 THEME words of this character (their
  trade or nature: herbs and potions for a herb witch, blood for a vampire) and fail if any bubble has one
  as a keyword; fail on everyday words («дом», «дома», "home", "people") and on a word in two bubbles. Try 2
  player messages with try_phrase (one with a bubble's plain word, one ordinary like «я дома, устал» that
  should bring nothing) and say what each brought.
- Only when an existing card was reworked: compare with THE CARDS AS THEY WERE BEFORE. Every proper name
  (people, ships, places, ranks, organisations), fate (retired, dead, alive), family standing and relation
  stays exactly (quote each change, e.g. «Henry Grey» became «Kenneth Grey»), and every fact of the old card
  is in the new core or a bubble (name any lost one).
